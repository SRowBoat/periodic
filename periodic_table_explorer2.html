Understanding Orbital Blocks ($s, p, d, f$)

The periodic table's distinctive layout is directly dictated by quantum mechanics and electron shell structure. Elements are divided into four orbital blocks based on which subshell receives its highest-energy valence electrons.

s-Block 2 e⁻ max

Groups 1, 2, plus Hydrogen & Helium. Spherical orbital shape. Contains reactive alkali & alkaline earth metals.

p-Block 6 e⁻ max

Groups 13 to 18. Dumbbell-shaped orbitals. Includes nonmetals, halogens, metalloids, and noble gases.

d-Block 10 e⁻ max

Groups 3 to 12. Clover-shaped orbitals. Transition metals filling $3d, 4d, 5d,$ and $6d$ subshells.

f-Block 14 e⁻ max

Inner Transition metals (Lanthanides & Actinides). Complex multi-lobed orbitals filling $4f$ and $5f$ subshells.
